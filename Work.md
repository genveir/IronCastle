# Iron Valley: Doing the Work

## Default assumptions about work

- **Money is no object.** Nobody works for cash. There is more than enough for everyone. Nobody worries about rent, bills or food. People work because they draw satisfaction from their commitments, because they want to help, and for a hundred other reasons.
- **Resources and items are abstract.** Everything is measured by value. Carrots (2) is two favor worth of carrots, not two carrots.
- **Everything grows faster in the Valley.** The soil is extraordinarily fertile. Crops bear fruit in days. Livestock produce milk, eggs and wool faster than anywhere else.

Do not roll for boring work. Assume the dull parts happen in the background.

## Growing crops

Three steps: Make a Promise, Try Your Best!!, Reap the Benefits.

Crops have no fixed growing times. The number of progress boxes comes from the fiction via Make a Promise. Growing carrots is usually laid-back urgency. Complexity depends on the character's experience: an experienced farmer with proper tools faces a simple task, someone who has never held a hoe faces a puzzling one.

Any crop can grow year round. Growing what is in season is strongly recommended because in-season harvests give +1 resource per filled box in Reap the Benefits.

Seeds are assumed to be available and not expensive.

Recommended but not binding: only Try Your Best!! once per crop per day. Life is not all work.

Firm rule: crops do not need daily watering, do not wither from neglect, and do not die at a season change. A patch of lettuce can be grown as slowly as the player likes.

## Caring for livestock

Three steps, same as crops: Make a Promise, Try Your Best!!, Reap the Benefits.

The difference is that livestock are cared for, not grown. They do not need replanting and progress does not restart from nothing each time.

Animals will grow to be adults no matter if you made promises about them. Caring for them while they grow up pays in satisfaction, not products.

Livestock must actually be owned before a promise can be made about them. Find them wild, trade for them, or acquire them through the narrative. The chicken comes before the egg.

Bees and other producers of sweet syrupy goodness count as livestock.

Recommended but not binding: care for each animal only once a day. They have plans.

Livestock produce their resource all year round. Their season is when they produce extra.

Livestock do not have to be kept for their products. Spending time with an animal, or a pet, is a legitimate promise that pays out in satisfaction.

Full livestock lists, seasons, maturity times and the nine fantastical species are in the livestock document.

## Crafting

Four steps: gather materials, Make a Promise, Try Your Best!!, Reap the Benefits.

Crafting covers everything made by hand: cooking, blacksmithing, turning raw goods into items, turning items into other items, and the arts. Writing a book and fixing a mechanism share the same framework.

Materials must be in hand before starting. No cake without flour and eggs. No repaired shovel without the broken shovel and some lumber.

By default, crafting assumes intermediate steps happen automatically. Wheat can be an ingredient in a cake because the character is assumed to mill it into flour along the way. Players who want to craft every stage manually may do so, and the game notes that doing it the long way produces items worth much more favor, so there is a mechanical reward for the effort. This flexibility is fully intended and can be applied selectively.

Crafting almost always uses the crafted items option in Reap the Benefits:

**Total value of ingredients x filled progress boxes = value of item**

## Foraging, fishing, catching bugs and finding inspiration

Two approaches.

**The easy way.** Use Root Around. One move, no promise needed. Either search for something specific, which risks finding nothing, or wander leisurely, which always yields something but less of it and not necessarily what was wanted.

**The hard way.** Make a Promise, Try Your Best!!, Reap the Benefits. Slower, but pays out much more. This is how to land a legendary fish that only appears on rainy days.

Ideas and Inspiration are an abstract resource found in the wilderness. They can be used to create works of art, invent tools, or be given as gifts. Some Townies have ideas as their favored gift, and ideas can only be gained by spending time in nature.

The game has no list of bugs and fish. Generate them with the Focus and Descriptor oracles, or with the Size, Creature Role and Creature Feature oracles.

## Having a job

Jobs exist for connection and interest, never for money. Every job pays handsomely regardless of what it is or how little gets done. Even the most niche shop always makes enough, because the community supports it.

Getting a job requires only asking. There is no roll. Formal applications are rare and resumes are considered a waste of paper. A character is more likely to have a job thrust upon them than to have to seek one.

Never roll to go to work. Assume the boring parts happen in the background.

Work is measured in projects, not hours. Think of jobs the way a sitcom treats them: the characters have jobs, but the audience only sees the interesting parts. Baking a cake for a wedding is a project. Wrangling a band of raccoons is a project. Filing is not.

Projects work exactly like promises: Make a Promise, Try Your Best!!, Reap the Benefits.

Days off are fine. Nobody in the Valley wants a job treated like a city job.

The job option in Reap the Benefits allows mixing favor with a coworker and resources or items, which makes work a good route to friendship.
