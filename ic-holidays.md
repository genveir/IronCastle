# Holidays and the Calendar

Fetch this when the calendar approaches a holiday.

## The calendar

The week is five days. A season is five weeks, so 25 days. A year is four seasons, so 100 days.

Days move faster here than in our world, which is why the week is short. The names are the settlement's own doing: long ago the people agreed to make each day of the week the focus of one of their tasks, so that they would never go a week without doing everything that mattered.

**Bread Day.** The first day. In the days of the founding, every family baked the week's bread, always making extra for visitors or people in need. It is still tradition to bake on the first day, though many read it as "getting the bread", meaning getting to work. The most serious workers are up before dawn on bread day.

**Friends Day.** The second day. After a good day of work one ought to spend a day in the company of good friends. Most social holidays fall on the second day of the week. A good day for a friend date, a sleepover, or doing something nice for someone you love.

**Soup Day.** The third day. By midweek the bread had gone a little stale, and there is no better thing to do with hard bread than dip it in soup. It is customary to make soup midweek, and many people look forward to it. It may be the hump day, but at least there is soup.

**Laundry Day.** The fourth day. Leave laundry to the end of the week and it does not get done, so people decided to do laundry and bathe on the fourth day. Bathing broke free of the rule over time, but laundry and other household chores remain the focus.

**Rest Day.** The fifth and final day. After a week of hard work, good friends, good food and chores, there is nothing left but rest. Rest in all forms: reading in the bathtub, walking in nature, painting by the water, playing board games at the pub. Some unfortunately have to work on rest day, but at least not for long.

## Spring Holidays

Most people arriving in the Valley do so during spring, and find themselves revitalised by the energy of the season. The grass is green, the flowers are blooming, life and colour are slowly returning to the world. This is a great chance to make a new beginning, and the spring holidays reflect that belief.

### Community Garden Day (Spring 6)

The second bread day of every season is a community garden day. Everyone works on the local community garden: planting, watering, harvesting, or fixing up the place. Do not let that fool you. A lot of work gets done, but there is also a lot of leisure. Music and dancing are easy to find, and so is the food.

*Preferred gift: spring crops.*

Iron Castle reading: the farmed ground between the curtain walls.

### Blossom Day (Spring 12)

By the third week of spring the colour has fully engulfed the Valley. Trees are filled with blooming flowers, the fields are a mosaic of vivid hues, there is love and life in the air. Blossom day celebrates life's return, a victory fanfare at the defeat of winter's grasp. People set up picnics and take in the beauty around them, and the food.

*Preferred gift: a bento, a packed meal for the picnic.*

### Spring Cleaning (Spring 14)

With life and colour back, it is time to shake off the cobwebs and clean. It is not uncommon for the whole town to help, one house at a time. When a whole town works together, chores get done in a jiffy.

*Preferred gift: a tasty drink to stay hydrated during the cleaning blitz.*

### Flea Market (Spring 16)

Cleaning up the whole town unearths a great many things nobody has use for, so why not hold a flea market. This is a haggler's heaven. Everyone has a table, a booth, or a tarp on the floor full of knick-knacks. And the deals.

*Preferred gift: something cool you found at the flea market.*

See the Flea Market oracle for stock.

### Candy Day (Spring 22)

More than eating sweets. It is about spending time with people you love, showing them you care, and maybe revealing your affections for a certain lovely person. It is also about sweets. Sugary ambrosia, taffy, pops, milk chocolate, bars, bonbons, chocolate covered nuts, chocolate everything.

*Preferred gift: unsurprisingly, chocolate.*

## Summer Holidays

The weather grows warmer and the days grow longer, and this time of year inflicts everyone with a sudden surge of energy. It feels like the days will not stop. The energy is contagious.

### Sports Day (Summer 2)

With so much energy going around, there is nothing left to do but tire ourselves out. Tournaments all day for every sport imaginable. It is not uncommon to enter several, even without knowing the rules. There is no greater glory than winning every trophy, a full sweep, a feat done once and never recreated.

*Preferred gift: an energizing drink or snack.*

### Community Garden Day (Summer 6)

Like its spring cousin, a chance for everyone to work the land together. With the jolt of energy the season provides, it is not uncommon to work until dusk without realising, but once the work is done the party begins.

*Preferred gift: summer crops.*

### Beach Party (Summer 15)

Around the middle of summer the ocean's waters are at the perfect temperature, so everyone agrees to spend the day at the beach. The high energy of the season is still in effect, but this is not all sport tournaments, though it would be silly not to expect a few. There are picnics, sand castles, swimming, and a lot of good food. This is a party, so expect music and dancing.

*Preferred gift: a hydrating drink.*

Iron Castle reading: there is no ocean. Run this as a river party on the bank below the bluff. See the open water question in ic-campaign-iron-castle.

### Town's Cook-Off (Summer 17)

Near the end of the month, what many consider the most important contest of all. Every year three judges are randomly picked from the entire town, and the goal is to wow these unlikely gourmands with palatable works of culinary art. Or something that tasted real good.

*Preferred gift: a dish made with passion.*

### Fishing Tournament (Summer 23)

During the last week of summer the waters fill with life as fish migrate from one region to another. Some prefer to watch from a safe distance, others partake in the ancient craft of a fishing tourney. Whoever catches the biggest honker is declared the winner and earns the title of master angler, along with a medal passed down for generations. Return the fishies to the water afterwards. They are going places.

*Preferred gift: fish.*

Iron Castle reading: the river below the bluff.

## Fall Holidays

With the energy of summer waning, the industrious spirit takes over. The leaves change, the weather grows colder, the days shrink, and everyone feels time beginning to run out, so they take their passions seriously. So much of this season is about making things.

### Community Garden Day (Fall 6)

The most productive day you will ever see. With the industrious spirit of fall in them, people improve the community garden by showing their craftiness first and foremost. There are people planting, watering and harvesting, but you have never seen so many tool boxes in your life. These holidays often end with a stage built on a whim, a band playing as the sun sinks, and one enormous feast.

*Preferred gift: fall crops.*

### Starfall Day (Fall 15)

Every year on the fifteenth of fall the night sky fills with streaks of every colour as tiny stars shoot across the darkness. The Valley is positioned just perfectly to take in the sights, so much so that this is a great period for tourism. The place gets dressed up for its guests, stalls pop up with food and games, and people wait patiently for the show. Many believe a night like this is perfect for revealing your feelings to your crush.

*Preferred gift: small accessories, such as rings and necklaces.*

Iron Castle reading: a fortress on a bluff is an exceptional vantage point. The walls and towers fill with people.

### Harvest Moon (Fall 17)

The night the moon is largest in the sky. Traditionally this moon let farmers work their land late into the night, and the people would work together to bring in the harvest. Nowadays it is a night of good company and jolly comradery, spent at the pub or the centre of town with drink, food, song and cheer. There is a bittersweet air, for the hard work of the last two seasons is soon to be rewarded with the cold of winter. Nevertheless people share in laughter, cherishing the moment before it is gone.

*Preferred gift: a baked good or a bottle of spirits.*

### Costume Day (Fall 19)

After nearly a year of work, a holiday about being a little silly. All Townies are expected to don a costume, surely worked on all season, and walk around as though in a fun-house mirror reflection of their lovely town. And there is candy. It is tradition to give candy to everyone you run into. Some believe costumes ought to be cute, others think they ought to be scary. Expect a candy fight.

*Preferred gift: candy, duh.*

### Harvest Feast (Fall 22)

The big one. The biggest, most important day of the year, and the thing the Valley is known for far and wide. If Starfall Day brought tourists, this is a shock. The festival to end all festivals. Food, art performance, a night market, a cooking contest, livestock contests, a speech by the town Mayor, and the coronation of the Harvest Spirit. Every year one member of the town is chosen by vote to wear the Fall Garland and take the honorary title of Harvest Spirit. This tradition is as old as the town, and the highest honour one can achieve in the Valley.

*Preferred gift: animal products and fall crops.*

Iron Castle reading: the speech is given by Jerome, Baron of Iron. Note the resonance: the castle already has a spirit that people half believe in, and this holiday crowns one by vote.

## Winter Holidays

Winter is as long as the other three seasons but feels longer. Life is as busy as ever, there is as much going on, and yet the world feels like it is slowing down. The holidays carry a sense of finality and a joyous fanfare.

### Community Garden Day (Winter 6)

You would think there would not be much to do at the community garden this season, and you would be wrong. There is snow everywhere, tubers popping out of the earth, and fences to fix. So much work to do, and the cold does not stop anyone. As with the other community garden days, this one is about working together and then sharing in good company. Sometimes a snowball fight breaks out.

*Preferred gift: winter crops.*

### Arts Festival (Winter 15)

With the temperature lowering, people want to spend time indoors, and what better way than a festival celebrating the arts. Grab your guitar, pick up your paint brush, start thinking of what to write, because everyone is expected to submit something. There will be food and drink, and the warmth of company as people walk into the local theater or take a seat in the centre of town.

*Preferred gift: a work of art.*

Iron Castle note: this is Bron's holiday. Art is his passion and the thing he is now ordinary at.

### Founding Day (Winter 17)

The year might almost be over, but there is enough time to celebrate the day that brought this town together. Founding Day celebrates the people of the Valley and the origins of the place they call home. Do not expect a history lesson, expect a historic party. People honour their ancestors with music, dancing, and the sharing of old family stories. You will never learn more about someone than you will on this day. Listen to their stories and share yours, for we are more than strangers in the cold. We are family.

*Preferred gift: a warm drink to fend off the cold.*

Iron Castle note: a natural place to learn about the castle's spirit and its past.

### Soupmas (Winter 23)

The origin of soupmas is often disputed, whether it came from an old yearly ritual or a legend nobody really knows. The only thing for certain is that a bowl of soup in the cold of winter lifts the soul. In the final days of winter, people gather around the soupmas tree and set up a long dining table, long enough for every Townie to sit at. And they do, because what is before them is a feast of the heart and the tummy. One by one, the people hand a bowl of soup to their secret soupmas buddy, and together they share laughter, surprises, and pippin' hot bowls of soup.

*Preferred gift: believe it or not, it's soup.*

### New Years Eve (Winter 25)

The final hours of the year. You have come a long way. You have made friends, maybe more than friends, and you have gotten work done. Hopefully not too much work. Life is not all about work. Life is about the memories we make. As the fireworks start going off in the sky, reflect on the year behind and the year ahead. This is not the end. It is the beginning.

*Preferred gift: clothing and toys.*

Iron Castle reading: substitute bonfires on the towers and lanterns from the walls if fireworks feel wrong for the setting.
