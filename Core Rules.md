# Iron Castle: Core Rules

## What this game is

Iron Valley is a solo roleplaying game by M. Kirin, built on Ironsworn and Ironsworn: Starforged by Shawn Tomkin. This version (Iron Castle) has been heavily modified by the player. 

The player takes the role of someone moving into a small community and starting their life anew. Its inspirations are life and farming simulation games: Animal Crossing, Story of Seasons (Harvest Moon), and Stardew Valley.

This is a cozy, low-stakes game. There is no combat, no hit points, no health, no harm, no death. Characters are not heroes. The only antagonist is time.

Two forces drive play: the mechanics (the rules) and the fiction (the story created while playing). Both matter equally. When in doubt, follow the fiction.

## Tone

The Valley is warm, inclusive and hopeful. Everyone is welcome. Nobody goes hungry, nobody worries about rent, and money is barely worth mentioning. People help each other because they want to.

The register of play is small and domestic: a good meal, a long chat, a chore done well, a gift that lands. Failure is rarely disaster. Failure usually means losing track of time, and losing track of time is often how the best things happen.

Do not roll for boring things. Assume the character handles the dull parts off screen.

## The flow of play

- Roleplay and envision what the character does.
- Whenever a move is triggered, play it out.
- Whenever the character commits to a goal, whether helping someone or getting work done, always begin with Make a Promise.
- When attempting something difficult, or working towards a promise, use Try Your Best!! and do an action roll. Mark progress as instructed.
- When enough progress boxes are filled, use Reap the Benefits to close that chapter.
- When told to use Time Passes, mark 1 tick on the current calendar day. On the fourth tick the day ends.
- When unsure what happens next, Ask the Spirit of the Castle.

## Stats

Every character has five stats.

- Edge: speed and agility.
- Heart: communication and empathy.
- Iron: strength and endurance.
- Shadow: mischief, stealth, and magic.
- Wits: intellect and reasoning.

## The action roll

1. Roll the action die (1d6) and add the relevant stat. Add +1 if a relevant skill applies. The total is the action score.
2. Roll the challenge dice (2d10) and compare the action score to each.
3. Action score beats both challenge dice: strong hit.
4. Action score beats one challenge die: weak hit.
5. Action score beats neither: miss.

In general terms:

- Strong hit: you succeed and make good time.
- Weak hit: you succeed but lose track of time.
- Miss: you fail and lose track of time.

Always refer to the specific move for what each outcome actually does.

## Matches

When both challenge dice show the same number, that is a match. Use matches to spice up the narrative. Make a success sweeter or a failure more interesting.

## The oracle roll

To roll on an oracle, roll 2d10 in order. The first die is the tens digit and the second is the units digit. Treat a 10 as a 0. Two tens is 100.

## Progress tracks and ticks

Make a Promise instructs the player to draw a number of progress boxes, up to a maximum of ten. Boxes are filled left to right and each box holds four ticks.

When marking more than one tick, carry over into the next box. If a tick would go into a box that was not drawn, draw it now. If the tenth box fills, ignore all leftover ticks.

A promise does not need every box filled to be fulfilled. Fewer filled boxes simply means a smaller reward.

If all drawn boxes fill and the promise is still not done, either draw new boxes and keep going, or treat the promise as one stage of a larger task, count it fulfilled, take the reward, and Make a Promise for the next stage.

## Satisfaction

Satisfaction is a small reserve of goodwill: people notice when you do good things for the castle. At any time, 5 satisfaction can be traded for 1 favor with any Townie.

## Learning skills

Skills are learned by doing. A character learns a new skill, or improves one they have, by fulfilling a promise that genuinely exercised it, through Reap the Benefits. The skill takes effect immediately. There is no advancement currency, no experience and no levelling. 

## Time

The only scarce resource is time. Each day holds four ticks. Time Passes marks one. When the fourth is marked, the day ends and a new one begins.

A week is five days. A season is five weeks, so 25 days. A year is four seasons, so 100 days.

The days of the week are Bread Day, Friends Day, Soup Day, Laundry Day, Rest Day.

The seasons are Spring, Summer, Fall, Winter.

## Iron Valley is not Ironsworn

From Ironsworn and Starforged, the following are absent: experience, progress rolls, health, harm, spirit, stress, supply, bonds, momentum, challenge ranks, conditions, legacy tracks, and impacts. Vows are called promises. Assets are reworked into skills. Progress is always marked as single ticks.

## Retcon

If anything in play makes the player uncomfortable or simply is not fun, stop, rewind, and change it. Decide what happened instead, or advance time and never mention it again. Then resume play. This is always allowed.
